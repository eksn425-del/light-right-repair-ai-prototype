# ICORES 2027: second-stage Regular Paper check
Verified 19 October 2026 against the official conference website. No account, submission, payment, or external communication action taken.

## Deadline
[Important Dates](https://icores.scitevents.org/ImportantDates.aspx) lists second-stage Regular Papers alongside Position Papers:
- Initial submission: 22 October 2026.
- Notification: 4 December 2026.
- Camera-ready and registration: 18 December 2026.

Exact official excerpts: “Paper Submission: October 22, 2026”; “All dates refer to AOE time, i.e. ‘Anywhere on Earth’”.

The page does NOT publish an explicit clock time. Its [linked AoE explanation](https://time.is/Anywhere_on_Earth) establishes UTC−12. End-of-day AoE would correspond to 23 October 2026 at 11:59:59 UTC; this is a conventional end-of-day interpretation, not a separately verified portal cutoff. Submit before that, ideally well before the deadline.

## Initial review vs final publication
[Submission Guidelines](https://icores.scitevents.org/Guidelines.aspx):
- Regular Papers represent completed, validated research; English PDF; anonymized double-blind review.
- Exact size excerpt: “10,000 to 50,000 characters (excluding white spaces), including references, tables, graphs and appendices.” Out-of-range submissions may be withheld from review.
- No separate initial-review page cap is stated. Publication limits are 25 pages for accepted Full Papers or 20 for accepted Short Papers, with at most four paid extra pages.
- Remove author details, identifying references, and acknowledgments. Check metadata and identifying links as practical precautions, not separately enumerated official requirements.
- Do not publicly post the submitted paper from initial submission until final selection results.
- Original work; avoid substantial overlap with published or concurrently submitted proceedings/journal papers; avoid excessive self-citation; no more than nine authors.
- AI-generated text requires acknowledgment disclosure and citations to the AI system in affected sections.
- Submit via PRIMORIS; successful submission generates an automatic email confirmation.

[Templates](https://icores.scitevents.org/Templates.aspx) are “strongly advised” for review and “mandatory” for camera-ready. The official link leads to [Springer computer-science proceedings instructions/templates](https://link.springer.com/series/558/information-for-authors-and-editors), which expressly covers CCIS and offers LaTeX2e and Word templates. Use that current Springer template rather than an old conference-specific SCITEPRESS template.

[FAQ](https://icores.scitevents.org/FAQ.aspx), sections 3.7, 3.13 and 4.1: replacement uploads are allowed before the submission deadline; only the current PRIMORIS version is reviewed; updates stop after the deadline. Its camera-ready answer describes 15–25 pages for accepted Full Papers and 15–20 for Short Papers. This wording is camera-ready-specific and must not be promoted to an initial-review minimum.

[Deadlines and Policies](https://icores.scitevents.org/ImportantInformation.aspx) requires camera-ready, copyright document, registration, and payment by the applicable early-registration deadline. These are later acceptance-stage requirements.

## AI policy and unresolved placement conflict
[AI Tools](https://icores.scitevents.org/AiTools.aspx) covers AI-generated text, figures, images, and code. Exact excerpt: “The AI tool must be named”. Disclosure must identify affected portions and briefly explain use. Authors remain accountable; AI is not an author. Language correction, shortening, translation and literature-review assistance are allowed, with source verification. AI cannot fabricate/manipulate research results. Generated illustrations require tool/prompt disclosure in captions and clear labeling; misleading scientific image changes are forbidden.

Important unresolved conflict: Guidelines tell review authors to remove the acknowledgments section, while AI policy directs AI disclosure into acknowledgments (and elsewhere permits an appropriate section). Neither page specifies a definitive anonymous-review exception. A non-identifying AI-use statement in an appropriate section, plus required citations, is a reasonable proposed reconciliation, NOT confirmed conference instruction. Ask the secretariat how to place disclosure if needed; do not silently omit required disclosure or declare acknowledgment placement settled. Official contact: icores.secretariat@insticc.org (linked on the conference homepage).
