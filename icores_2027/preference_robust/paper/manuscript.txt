Preference-Robust Screening and Decision
Support for Solar-Access Interventions in Urban
Renewal

Abstract. An architect assessing solar-access interventions needs alternatives that remain useful before priorities are agreed. A fixed weighted
top ten can hide this dependence. We study 630 two-building heightreduction rules in a canonical urban block and replace reliance on one
declared score with preference-space analysis and a ten-candidate regretbased shortlist. Newly low-sun area is zero throughout this rule universe, leaving three discriminating criteria: low-sun-area reduction, mean
direct-sun gain and nominal removed volume. A frozen protocol uses
three preference spaces, each with 20,000 training, test and replication
weights. A greedy sampled-minimax heuristic is evaluated independently and checked by continuous linear-programming audits. On the fullsimplex test, mean absolute utility regret falls from 0.010759 for the
historical top ten to 0.000054; winner coverage rises from 77.695% to
97.940%. Continuous worst-case regret falls from 0.246359 to 0.007656.
Both sets already have zero regret in the solar-access-priority and local
spaces, and the new set recovers fewer members of preference-specific top
tens in every space. Five candidates change; three new shortlist members lack archived Honeybee review. Historical simulation and geometry
evidence remains separate from these new preference experiments. The
contribution is a reproducible, evidence-linked application of established
multicriteria and regret ideas to architectural screening, not a new general optimization algorithm or an architect-validated preference model.
AI assistance is disclosed [9].

Multicriteria analysis · Urban renewal

Introduction

Which interventions should an architect examine when the relative importance
of sunlight improvement and building removal has not yet been agreed?1 A numerical ranking is easy to produce once weights have been chosen. Its apparent
precision can nevertheless obscure an unresolved decision: how much area benefit, additional exposure and removal cost should count against one another? In
existing urban blocks this matters before expensive geometric development, professional simulation and stakeholder consultation. The purpose of screening is to
preserve useful opportunities for that later decision, rather than to announce a
universally best building intervention.

We examine a bounded case with 45 canonical building groups, 36 eligible
groups and all 630 unordered pairs of eligible groups. Each pair receives a fixed
height-reduction rule. A historical screening score assigns weights 45%, 35%,
15% and 5% to normalized low-sun-area reduction, mean direct-sun gain, lower
nominal removal volume and avoidance of newly low-sun area. These weights
are researcher-declared preferences. They have not been elicited from architects,
derived by an analytic hierarchy process or learned from observed choices. Moreover, the fourth metric is zero for every rule candidate, so its term is constant
and cannot distinguish the 630 options.
The present research asks whether a ten-candidate choice set can retain valuable alternatives across explicit preference conditions without treating those historical weights as authoritative. It separates two outputs. Rank acceptability
describes how often each candidate occupies a rank under an assumed distribution of weights. Shortlist regret measures how much of the best achievable
utility is lost when the architect can choose only among the retained alternatives. A set that preserves the winning opportunity need not reproduce all ten
highest-ranked alternatives. This distinction proves central to the results.
Three contributions follow. First, we formulate a finite, constrained solarintervention decision model with explicit performance semantics and an effective
three-criterion utility. Second, we implement and evaluate a preference-robust
screening procedure using separated training, test and replication scenarios,
budget-matched baselines and a continuous worst-case audit. Third, we connect each selected pair to its actual simulation and translated-geometry evidence,
including missing review. The methodological ingredients have established precedents; the contribution lies in their particular integration and controlled empirical assessment for this architectural task.
The results support opportunity preservation under broad weight uncertainty, but not unconditional replacement of the historical shortlist. Under the full
simplex, a new greedy set sharply reduces regret. Under ordered solar-accesspriority and local historical preferences, the historical set already preserves an
optimal candidate, so there is no regret improvement to claim. The robust set
also sacrifices recovery of preference-specific top-ten membership. These findings
make the decision objective more transparent: protecting at least one attractive
option and offering many highly ranked similar options are different services.
All new experiments use archived candidate performances. They do not rerun Honeybee/Radiance, observe the physical site or survey architects. Historical
computational-efficiency, selective simulation and geometry-correction evidence
supports the workflow, with its original limits. Preference robustness here concerns changing linear weights on fixed performance values. It does not establish
robustness to weather, geometric error, ownership constraints, structural feasibility or construction cost.

Related Work and Methodological Position

2.1

Uncertain Preferences and Representative Subsets

SMAA-2 extends stochastic multicriteria acceptability analysis to rank positions,
allowing the analyst to examine alternatives over weight space rather than selecting one weight vector [6].2 Monte Carlo approximation of these acceptability
integrals is an established implementation approach [11]. We use this restricted
idea with fixed criterion values and explicitly declared weight distributions. We
do not implement uncertain performance distributions, central weight vectors or
confidence factors, and do not equate acceptability with the probability that a
real architect likes a candidate.
Robust ordinal regression instead retains additive value functions compatible
with decision-maker comparisons, distinguishing necessary from possible preferences [4]. No such comparisons are available in this study. Consequently, our
weight restrictions are research scenarios, not a fitted ordinal-regression model.
The distinction also prevents treating unanimous sampled rankings as necessary
preferences over a continuous domain. A future elicitation stage could constrain
the admissible model set using actual architect judgments; it is not part of the
present evidence.
Minimax regret compares the utility of a decision against the best feasible
alternative under compatible utility functions. Boutilier et al. [1] develop optimization and elicitation methods for this criterion. Our output is a subset
from which a later decision can be made, rather than one irreversible choice.
Regret-minimizing representative databases already formalize bounded subsets
whose best attainable utility approximates that of the full database [7]. Their
relative-regret setting and algorithmic guarantees should not be transferred to
our absolute normalized loss or greedy implementation. Neither regret-based
subset selection nor exploration of weight space is claimed as an invention here.
This positioning determines the evaluation. A transparent heuristic must
state the objective it actually optimizes; high individual acceptability is not the
same objective as collective opportunity preservation. A sampled maximum is
not a certified continuous maximum. A fixed-set linear-programming audit can
examine the latter without proving that the set itself is globally optimal. We
retain each distinction in the experimental design and interpretation.
2.2

Architectural and Environmental Decision Support

Building-performance optimization already covers multiobjective design, computational efficiency, uncertainty and adoption challenges [8]. EvoMass connects
optimization-based building-massing exploration with typological interpretation
and designer reconfiguration [12]. Its scope is broader than the fixed intervention rules considered here. It provides relevant architectural context, but its
user-study evidence does not validate this workflow or our preferences. We do
not claim the first architect-in-the-loop optimization process.

Solar-performance assessment also uses different physical quantities. Urban
solar and daylight availability [2] and the taxonomy of solar planning metrics [3]
motivate careful naming of the outcome. Our direct-sun duration and threshold area are not indoor illuminance, annual irradiation or regulatory compliance.
Ladybug [10], Radiance [13] and pvlib [5] provide tool context, rather than guarantees about this case’s input geometry or simulation recipe. The new research

where Wtrain concatenates 60,000 training draws. We use a greedy heuristic,
called Robust10. Starting with best utility zero, at each step choose the remaining candidate yielding the smallest maximum training regret. Exact ties

recorded and evaluated to expose how much each additional slot contributes.
We do not present the greedy sequence as a ranking of individual architectural
merit: its second addition is chosen conditional on its first, and so forth.

Continuous Audit, Replication and Sampling Error

The continuous audit in Table 4 confirms that the full-space historical maximum
is 0.246359 at the pure-volume vertex (0, 0, 1). Robust10’s maximum is 0.007656,
with an interior-edge witness approximately (0.211837, 0, 0.788163) and competing candidate C13/C29. Checking only the three simplex vertices would miss
this robust worst case. The grid maximum is 0.007576 and the test maximum is
0.007539, both below the continuous value as expected. The audit certifies the
fixed-set loss within numerical tolerances; it does not certify a globally optimal
ten-candidate set.
The unrestricted 41-member A/H/V front has zero regret throughout all
three continuous spaces. This is consistent with nonnegative linear utility: a
dominated candidate cannot uniquely improve the maximum. Its 41 alternatives
exceed the review budget, so this is an opportunity reference rather than a fair
ten-candidate superiority claim. The unrestricted 12-member A/V front still
has full-space worst regret 0.232558 at pure duration preference; extra members
cannot repair an omitted criterion.
Independent full-space replication gives mean regret 0.011351 for Historical10 and 0.000055 for Robust10, with winner coverage 0.76635 and
0.97885, respectively. The paired robust-minus-historical mean regret difference on the test split is −0.010705, with a conditional Monte Carlo 95% interval [−0.011132, −0.010279]; replication gives −0.011296 and
[−0.011736, −0.010855]. Ordered and local replication retain zero regret for
both sets. Thus the prespecified criterion of no larger mean and 95th-percentile
regret in every space, with a strict improvement somewhere, is met. This
is improvement in the full space and equality in the other two, not strict
improvement everywhere.
Across all 630 candidates, the largest absolute test–replication top-tenacceptability difference is 0.01015 in the full space, 0.00725 in the ordered space
and 0.00920 locally. The full-space maximum winner-acceptability difference is
0.00650; both restricted spaces have zero winner difference. These diagnostics
support the broad qualitative pattern, not the significance of each last decimal.
No simultaneous confidence claim or physical error bound follows from the
sampling precision.
All ten greedy additions reduce the training maximum, from 0.341577 at
the first candidate to 0.007269 at ten. It never reaches zero. The final set is
not padded with slots after achieving perfect coverage. Nonetheless, individual
greedy choices can be redundant in some preference regions, and no exchangebased or exact shortlist optimizer is compared. The measured improvement belongs to this frozen heuristic, training distribution and case, not to an optimality
theorem.

Integration with Simulation and Geometry Review

6.1

A Decision Workflow with Distinct Evidence Gates

The intended workflow begins with an urban-block model, explicit eligibility and
a fixed intervention rule.6 Fast exposure evaluation produces the complete metric
table. Preference analysis then produces rank distributions and alternative tencandidate sets. The architect examines their trade-offs and review status before
requesting additional simulation or developing geometry. Figure 2 separates these
activities.
A practical first conversation is whether the full simplex is appropriate. If a
project team rules out volume-dominant weights and accepts wA ≥ wH ≥ wV ,
the result already establishes zero fixed-set regret for Historical10 over that continuous support. If priorities remain open, Robust10 retains more opportunities,
but the team must budget for three previously unreviewed rule candidates and
eight missing translations. If the objective is to offer multiple similar high-benefit
options, top-ten recovery can be more relevant than winner coverage. The interface should expose these choices rather than display one unqualified “best”
list.
Candidate rejection for ownership, structural or heritage reasons changes
the feasible universe. Such exclusions cannot be treated as harmless after-thefact annotations: the best available utility and regret benchmark can change. A
revised analysis should record exclusions and recompute utilities and set evaluation against the revised feasible alternatives, with any normalization policy
made explicit. The frozen experiment remains a reference, not an authorization
to intervene in a building.

Historical evidence comprises 23 curated rule reviews and a disjoint, pre-frozen
20-candidate same-site coverage cohort. The curated pool combined historical
top-ten, area–volume, historical and reference choices; it was purposive. The
coverage cohort was selected for score strata, volume and identifier coverage
before its reported Honeybee outcomes, rather than randomly across sites. Its
identities and outcomes are not reselected in this work. There are 43 reviewed
pairs, or about 6.83% of the full universe, but agreement is reported by cohort.

sub-grid accuracy. A score correlation of one is not exact score agreement; only
nine coverage scores agree exactly. V is shared between routes and N is constant,
further limiting independence of the composite comparison. Cohort overlap at a
top-K cutoff depends on tie handling and cannot establish full-universe recall.

Discussion, Limitations and Reproducibility

7.1

What the New Evidence Supports

The strongest contribution is a changed decision contract.7 Historical fixed
weights answer which candidates score highly under one priority statement.
The new analysis asks how much choice is lost if only ten are retained before
priorities are settled. Its full-space results show that five substitutions can
preserve a much broader range of winning opportunities. The continuous audit
further establishes that the improvement is not merely the failure of random
sampling to encounter extreme weights.
The same evidence limits the claim. Restricted-space results show no need
for a new set to preserve an optimal utility opportunity. The decline in topten recovery shows that the set is not universally more representative. A single
candidate can secure zero regret for a region while other slots serve a different
region, leaving few locally high-ranked alternatives. Therefore the contribution
should be described as preference-robust opportunity screening under a specified
objective, not a superior architectural recommendation list in every sense.
There is also a validation cost. The historical shortlist benefited from curated
review, while three robust alternatives remain 2.5D-only. A workflow that quietly
calls those alternatives validated would confuse preference analysis with physical
evidence. Instead, the new shortlist tells the architect where further review is
needed. Prioritizing those missing reviews would be a concrete next experiment,
followed by re-evaluation if their performance values materially change. It cannot
be replaced by attaching the old cohort’s correlation to each new candidate.
7.2

Threats to Validity

Preference model. Additive normalized utility assumes compensation between
area, exposure and volume. Bounds and units shape its trade-offs. Uniform simplex area, the ordered restriction, local radius and equal-space mixture are research choices without architect elicitation. Correlated A and H may give sunlight improvement two related channels of influence. Other utility forms, vetoes
or explicit removal budgets could change selection. The present results do not
establish that any of these weights represent a target professional population.
Optimization and evaluation. Greedy construction is path dependent and is
not globally optimal. Historical-ranking compression makes AV10 and AHV10
useful transparent baselines but not strong optimized subset competitors. The
study lacks a swap heuristic, exact combinatorial benchmark or broader budget
search. The independent LP audit evaluates fixed sets on the chosen supports
only. Monte Carlo confidence intervals describe sampling precision conditional
on fixed performances; they do not cover model uncertainty, selection across
many reported quantities or differences between sites.

verified as-built footprints. Shared components, overlaps and OBJ differences can

Evidence coverage. Curated23 and coverage20 remain nonprobability samesite cohorts. Aggregate agreement can conceal spatial differences and cannot

translated-design results concern different geometries. Containment removes the
observed outside-footprint failure under tested conditions, but does not prove
structural feasibility, equal removal cost or general correctness of arbitrary
imported meshes. There is no new Honeybee experiment, architect survey,
realized intervention or measured decision-time saving.
Scope of generalization. All 630 alternatives share one rule family and a single
canonical block. Exactly two altered groups excludes many plausible interventions. Property rights, function, heritage, construction sequencing and social impacts are outside the objective vector. A shortlist suitable for this early screening
task may be unsuitable after those constraints are imposed. Replication weights
test numerical sampling stability, not external validity.
7.3

Reproducibility and Release Boundaries

The new package retains a pre-outcome protocol and input hashes; scripts and
software versions; every retained weight; all 630 rank histograms; candidate
memberships; scenario-level test, replication and grid results; greedy traces; continuous LP status and witnesses; and tables linked to saved CSVs. Historical
inputs and manuscripts remain unmodified. This enables re-execution of the
preference analysis from fixed aggregate performances, including unfavorable
outcomes.
The evidence distinguishes newly computed weight analysis, recomputed
archived tables, historically measured timing, historical geometry replays and
unchanged results reused for comparison. No newly computed utility is presented as a new physical observation. Missing rule review and missing translation are
separate fields. The archive also preserves negative results rather than dropping
candidate regions that reduce the strength of the narrative.
Raw CAD, DXF, SKP and OBJ inputs, receiver coordinates, Honeybee models, WEA carriers and Radiance intermediates remain restricted. The aggregate and algorithmic package does not confer permission to redistribute them or
fully reproduce site simulations. Anonymous review materials exclude identifying repository links. Any broader release requires rights and anonymity review.
These limitations reduce independent physical reproducibility even though the
new preference experiment is reproducible from the retained table.

Conclusion

A fixed top ten cannot answer every screening question when priorities are unresolved.8 For a finite universe of 630 solar-access height-reduction rules, we removed a constant risk metric from active weight discrimination, retained the historical score unchanged and evaluated three explicit preference spaces. A greedy
sampled-minimax ten-candidate set substantially improves full-simplex opportunity preservation, with mean held-out regret 0.000054 and continuous worst-case
regret 0.007656, compared with 0.010759 and 0.246359 for the historical set.
The result is conditional and informative in both directions. Historical10
already achieves zero regret under ordered solar-access-priority and local historical weights. Robust10 reduces top-ten membership recovery in all spaces and
introduces three candidates without archived rule-level Honeybee review. These
outcomes distinguish preserving a good eventual choice from reproducing a familiar ranking. They also identify concrete next work: eliciting actual project
preferences, reviewing the newly exposed candidates and checking geometry before design commitments.
The contribution is an evidence-linked architectural decision-support application of established acceptability and regret concepts. It does not supply a new
general algorithm, validated architect preferences or physical-model robustness.
Fast enumeration, preference analysis, selective simulation and geometric checks
become useful together when their outputs remain distinguishable and the final
decision stays with the responsible professionals.
