            Solar-Access-Priority Screening:
        Supplementary Decision Experiment and
                   Revision Options



        Abstract. Solar-access repair should prioritize sunlight improvement
        before minimizing nominal building intervention. We test whether a ten-
        candidate shortlist can retain more high-ranked alternatives under that
        objective than a historical fixed-weight list. A prospectively frozen exper-
        iment uses 630 archived two-building height-reduction candidates, three
        constrained weight spaces, three minimum-area-benefit filters, and inde-
        pendent training, test and replication weights. Two acceptability-based
        selectors maximize empirical top-ten recovery, with or without protec-
        tion of observed training winners. None of six contexts satisfies the pre-
        declared conditions for replacing the historical method: recovery gains
        remain below 0.02, and some lists reduce mean area or duration benefits
        beyond the declared guard. Nevertheless, useful conditional effects occur.
        Under a 70% total sunlight-weight constraint, winner coverage increas-
        es from 89.08% to 100%, and continuous worst-case normalized regret
        decreases from 0.019380 to zero. Under ordered weights, the historical
        list already has zero regret. A stringent area filter leaves only twelve
        candidates and conceals opportunity loss if evaluated only within that
        subset. These are new decision analyses of archived performances, not
        new physical simulations. This supplementary report preserves the prior
        manuscript and provides evidence-based revision choices; it is not a re-
        placement submission manuscript or a claim of a new general algorithm.
        AI assistance is disclosed [11].

        Keywords: Solar access · Decision support · Preference sensitivity ·
        Candidate screening · Negative results


1     Research Decision and Scope

The author has clarified the project objective: improve insufficient sunlight in a
dense existing urban block first, prefer smaller interventions second, and leave
final design selection to architects.1 This is a different decision contract from
unrestricted uncertainty over all benefit–cost weights. A method that protects a
very low-volume alternative with almost no sunlight benefit can be useful under
an open preference model while being poorly aligned with solar-access repair.
    The previous experiment used the same 630 archived candidates and found
that a greedy regret-oriented Robust10 improved opportunity preservation over
the full weight simplex. It also found that the historical top ten already had

    The present question is narrower: can ten review slots preserve more highly

preserving the prior formal manuscript. Retention is an archival decision, not an
endorsement of submitting that manuscript unchanged under the newly clarified
framing.
    The outcome is not “nothing improved.” Several selectors improve winner
coverage in duration-dominant sunlight scenarios, and all new lists improve or
match empirical recovery. The important distinction is between a measurable
conditional benefit and the stronger, predeclared claim that a new method merits
replacing the historical shortlist. Changing the success threshold after seeing
those small gains would erase that distinction.

2.2   What Is Established Mathematics?

MCDA provides a mature framework for multiple criteria and incomplete prefer-
ences [4]. SMAA-2 examines rank acceptability across preference conditions [6],
with established Monte Carlo implementations [13]. We use a restricted fixed-
performance version: sampled weight frequencies are properties of a declared
integration measure, not probabilities that real architects prefer an option.
    Robust ordinal regression uses preference information to restrict compatible
additive value functions [5]. No elicited pairwise architect comparisons are avail-
able here, so imposing weight inequalities is not fitting an ordinal-regression
model. Minimax regret and representative subsets are also established [1,9].
Their theoretical guarantees cannot be transferred automatically to this finite,
normalized absolute-regret implementation.
    Building-performance optimization, designer-led massing exploration and ur-
ban solar optimization all have substantial precedents [10,14,7,8]. The solar met-
rics literature distinguishes exposure, irradiation, daylight and their normaliza-
tion [3,2]. The possible contribution here is the explicit solar-priority decision
formulation, controlled comparison and connection to evidence states in one con-
strained intervention case. It is not the invention of SMAA, Pareto screening,
regret minimization, or architect-in-the-loop design.

3.1     Three Meanings of Sunlight Priority
The protocol was committed before any new threshold count, weight draw, rank-
ing or shortlist outcome.3 Let W = {w ≥ 0 : j wj = 1}. Three primary spaces

Ordered weights prioritize the normalized area coefficient over duration and vol-
ume. Total-sunlight constraints instead cap the volume coefficient while allowing
duration to dominate area. Neither formulation is strict lexicographic optimiza-
tion of sunlight, and neither guarantees a minimum physical area benefit. The
70% and 80% bounds are research scenarios, not industry standards.
    Three layered contexts use W70 after requiring A ≥ 90, 180 or 270 m2 . These
round multiples of the 9 m2 cell were frozen prospectively. They are minimum-
benefit scenarios, not regulations or surveyed expert thresholds. The surviving
universes contain 166, 50 and 12 candidates respectively, compared with 630 in
each unfiltered context. Raising a floor can remove an otherwise useful duration-
beneficial candidate; success within the smaller universe must not conceal that
loss.

Let T10 (w) be the deterministic preference-specific top ten. For a ten-member
set S, expected recovery is

Linearity of expectation makes this objective modular. Coverage10 therefore
takes the ten largest training top-ten counts. Ties use training winner count, then
smaller training mean rank, then natural pair identity. This is exact empirical
optimization of the stated objective, not a new general algorithm or proof of
optimal population recovery.
    ProtectedCoverage10 additionally requires every distinct deterministic train-
ing winner to be included. It fills the remaining slots in the same acceptability
order. When the required set has at most ten elements this exactly solves the
constrained empirical modular problem. If it had more than ten, the frozen rule
would declare infeasibility rather than invent a fallback. No test, replication,
grid or continuous-audit winner can change selection. In the actual run both
methods produce identical memberships in every context: the recovery-optimal
lists already contain the observed training winners. Thus there is no observed
incremental benefit to the protection constraint here.
    Recovery is different from any-top-ten coverage, winner coverage, architec-
tural diversity, or regret. For the eligible universe Dc ,

Conversely, maximizing recovery alone does not generally guarantee preservation
of every possible winner.

4.3   The Effect of Hard Minimum Benefits

A90 removes 464 candidates but leaves the historical and new Solar70 member-
ships unchanged. Its conditional comparison therefore matches Solar70. A180
leaves fifty candidates and changes several adapted baselines; ProtectedCover-
age10 matches AHV10 in this context. These are separate, explicitly filtered
decision universes.
     A270 leaves only twelve candidates. Historical, prior-robust, Coverage and
Protected adaptations become the same ten-member set, with test recovery
0.996625 and zero conditional regret. AV10 and AHV10 remain different and
have recovery 0.900000; they must not be described as identical to those four
lists. More importantly, the A270 filter itself has mean full-universe opportunity
loss about 0.030440 and maximum observed loss about 0.228607 on the Solar70
test weights. A duration-focused preference can lose a valuable candidate before
list selection begins. The near-perfect conditional recovery partly reflects a much
smaller choice universe, not a universally superior decision method.
     No threshold is selected as the “best” after seeing these outcomes. An archi-
tect might rationally impose a minimum area benefit, but must understand that
it expresses a substantive project requirement and may sacrifice duration oppor-
tunities. The present experiment exposes that choice rather than authorizing it
on the architect’s behalf.

5     Simulation and Geometry Remain Separate Evidence

The new computation consists only of decision analysis on archived A/H/V /N .5
No new Honeybee/Radiance simulation, physical survey, architect study or con-
struction assessment has been performed. The original 23 curated rule reviews
and frozen twenty-candidate same-site coverage sample are preserved without
reselection. They total 43 reviewed pairs, not validation of all 630 candidates.
    In the archived coverage cohort, area agreement has Spearman correlation
0.997668, with mean absolute difference 0.45 m2 caused by one 9 m2 discrepancy
among twenty pairs. This is not sub-grid physical accuracy. Historical score
agreement partly shares nominal V and constant N across routes. Honeybee and
the screening route also use different effective solar rays; the aggregate agreement
does not establish identical physics inputs or generalize to unreviewed pairs.
    Historical translated geometries are another stage. Four of five archived trans-
lations failed solid containment. Controlled corrections removed observed new
obstruction events under the frozen rays and receivers. For C9/C24 the corrected
translated area benefit is 351 m2 , whereas the rule-screening value is 396 m2 .
For C9/C15 the corrected translation retains 342 m2 , whereas its rule value is
351 m2 . Those values concern different geometry and cannot be interchanged in
the decision table.
    The previously measured implementation speed ratio of about 483 compares
two unindexed single-thread Python implementations under matched primitive
inputs. It supports the feasibility of rapid finite screening, not superiority over

6     Interpretation and Manuscript Revision Options
6.1     Answers to the Decision Questions
The new selectors are more explicit about a particular decision service: retaining
multiple high-ranked options across stated sunlight-priority preferences, rather
than accepting one unvalidated weight vector.6 This gives them interpretability,
but does not prove that they are generally better recommendations. In this
experiment the gain is too small, or sunlight trade-offs too large, to satisfy the
agreed replacement rule.
    If Historical10 already includes every winner, as in the ordered space, a new
set can add value only through other services such as retaining more near-best
options, reducing review burden or improving raw performance. Here the recov-
ery gain is small and mean duration declines beyond the guard. That is not a
compelling replacement case. Under Solar70 and Solar80, by contrast, Histori-
cal10 does miss some duration-priority winners. Adding those opportunities is a
real conditional benefit, but AHV10 and prior Robust10 also retain them, and
the new recovery improvement still misses the prospectively declared threshold.
    The old broad-space Robust10 lowers mean nominal volume but offers much
lower mean area benefit than Historical10 in the primary contexts (208.8 versus
309.6 m2 ). This does not make its earlier full-simplex regret result false. It shows
that unrestricted cost-dominant preferences answer a different question from
sunlight-first repair. The correct response is to align the preference model with
the project, rather than advertise the largest numerical improvement from an
unsuitable weight region.

6.2     What Should Be Retained or Revised?
Conservative route. Preserve the historical shortlist as the default review list,
with the newly measured sensitivity and evidence-state annotations. Describe
the research as solar-priority interpretable screening and decision support. Its
contribution is an explicit constrained decision model, controlled comparison
and empirical finding that a plausible new selector fails a stronger replacement
test. Do not claim that 45/35/15/5 has thereby become an expert-approved or
universally optimal choice.

6.3     Limitations and Reproducibility
The analysis uses one simplified site, one fixed rule family, a single day, a coarse
receiver grid and fixed archived performances. Weight uniformity, normaliza-
tion, benefit floors and decision gates are researcher-declared. Correlated sun-
light metrics remain compensatory; raw V omits actual cost, relocation, rights
and social effects. New weight seeds do not create new site evidence. Repeated
evaluation of multiple contexts does not turn conditional Monte Carlo intervals
into a familywise scientific discovery test.
    All comparisons preserve ten slots, independent selection/evaluation and un-
favorable results. The archive contains input hashes, the pre-outcome protocol,
software and run logs, retained weights, ranking memberships, candidate accept-
abilities, selection traces, continuous-audit witnesses, scenario metrics, stability
results and evidence status. The supplementary figures and tables are generated
directly from those saved outputs. Physical raw data and the private repository
remain restricted; no public replication right is asserted. Author checks of data
rights, originality, AI assistance, anonymity and submission declarations remain
required.


7     Conclusion
None of six prospectively declared contexts supports replacing Historical10 under
the complete frozen rule.7 Recovery improvements are below 0.02 in both inde-
pendent splits, and some context-specific lists sacrifice too much mean area or

retention in total-sunlight-weight spaces, including zero continuous condition-

make within-filter performance look excellent while excluding valuable duration-
focused opportunities.
    Architects should first clarify whether priority means ordered criteria, a to-

examine a small annotated set, compare its sensitivities, request missing sim-

experiment supports that transparent workflow and a cautious default, not an
invented algorithm breakthrough or construction-ready recommendation.
