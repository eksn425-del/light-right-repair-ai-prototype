# Pre-result methodological position

The author supplies a project objective, not empirically elicited weights: improve sunlight first and prefer smaller interventions second. Three distinct operationalizations must not be conflated.

1. Ordered weights constrain relative coefficients, but remain compensatory. They are not strict lexicographic maximization of sunlight.
2. A total sunlight weight of at least 0.70 or 0.80 still permits area-duration trade-offs and nonzero compensation with volume. The two bounds are scenario choices, not standards.
3. A minimum area-benefit filter is a veto before ranking, guaranteeing only that minimum. It is not proof of physically adequate light or architectural feasibility. All baselines must use the same feasible universe for within-filter comparisons; unchanged historical sets evaluated outside that universe need explicit treatment.

For a fixed distribution over weights and deterministic top-ten T(w), expected recovery is Q(S)=E[|S intersect T(w)|/10]=(1/10)sum_{i in S}P(i in T(w)). This is modular; choosing the ten largest empirical acceptabilities exactly maximizes its training estimate under an unconstrained ten-element budget. Additional constraints alter the feasible selection problem. No new general algorithm follows from this elementary identity.

Coverage of at least one member of T(w), coverage of a winning candidate, best-utility regret, and recovery of many top-ten members are different objectives. High recovery does not guarantee low worst-case regret, and zero regret can be achieved with only one relevant candidate while the other nine slots are poor for that preference region.

Report raw A/H/V and rule-HB coverage with ranked-set metrics. A gain in expected recovery is a conditional decision-service result, not proof that architects make better final designs. Independently sampled evaluation estimates performance under declared distributions; it does not validate those distributions as human preferences.

A future set-selection contribution can honestly be an explainable application and evaluation of established MCDA ideas. A null result can justify preserving the fixed shortlist and using sensitivity/feasibility analysis as an audit rather than replacing it.
