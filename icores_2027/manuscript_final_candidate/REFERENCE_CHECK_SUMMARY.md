## AI disclosure: placement interpretation remains open

The [AI policy](https://icores.scitevents.org/AiTools.aspx) requires the tool name, affected parts, and purpose. [Guidelines](https://icores.scitevents.org/Guidelines.aspx) additionally require AI-system citations in affected text sections. Both mention acknowledgments, but anonymous-review instructions remove acknowledgments; the AI policy also allows an appropriate section. The pages do not expressly reconcile this conflict.

A non-identifying dedicated disclosure plus truthful affected-section citations is a reasonable editorial interpretation, **not a confirmed venue ruling**. Preserve this caveat in the handoff. Disclosure must match actual assistance, not claim only proofreading if substantive drafting occurred. Scientific authors remain responsible.

- [Peherstorfer, Willcox, Gunzburger (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1082469), SIAM Review 60(3):550–591. Explicit lower-fidelity approximations coordinated with high-fidelity evaluations. Use to distinguish multifidelity modeling from exact same-model computational reuse; it does not validate this paper's exactness or speedup. - [Nguyen, Reiter, Rigo (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2013.08.061), Applied Energy 113:1043–1058. Supports building-optimization context, computational-efficiency concerns, algorithm choice, surrogates, and uncertainty. A review, not evidence that the present approach wins a benchmark.
- [Mueller, Ochsendorf (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.autcon.2015.02.011), Automation in Construction 52:70–82. Interactive evolution combines structural criteria with designer preferences through parent selection, mutation rate, and generation-size control. Supports preference-sensitive exploration, not objective validity of a chosen scalar utility.
